Your day stays on your computer

No account, and your tasks, notes and diary never leave your computer. Here is exactly what Pluto keeps, what it looks at, the one small count it sends, and what it never does.

Where your things live

Tasks, notes, your diary, routines, meetings and tracked time are saved in one database file, pluto.db, in Pluto's own folder on your computer:

  • Windows: %APPDATA%\app.pluto.desktop
  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/app.pluto.desktop

It is never uploaded. There is no Pluto account, and Pluto's server holds none of it: only the anonymous count described below.

What time tracking looks at

Time tracking is off until you switch it on. When it is on:

  • Apps: Pluto notes which app is in front, by its file name (for example Code.exe). Never the window title, never what is inside the window.
  • Websites: it reads the address bar of the browser in front and keeps only the site name (github.com). The rest of the address is dropped straight away. Private and incognito windows are skipped, and nothing is read while you type in the address bar.
  • Away from the keyboard: when you step away, tracking pauses.

The pet's senses

  • Types along with you (off by default): Pluto counts key presses so the pet can type along. It never knows which keys, and the count is not stored or sent.
  • Steps aside in full screen: Pluto asks the system whether something fills the screen. That is all it learns.
  • Tab Tantrum (off by default): Pluto counts the tabs in your browser so the pet can complain when there are too many.

Your diary

The diary is locked with your password, and you can lock it any time with Lock now. When you create the password, Pluto shows you a recovery key once; keep it, because it is the only way to set a new password without losing what you wrote.

Spot things to do is off until you switch it on. It reads your entries on your computer to underline lines like "I need to submit the essay by Friday". Assistants can never see the diary.

How we count people using Pluto

So we know how many people use Pluto, the app sends one small message a day to Pluto's server while it is open. It contains only:

  • A random install number made on your computer the first time Pluto opens. It isn't linked to your name, email, computer or anything else, and a new install gets a new one.
  • The app version (for example 0.6.1) and whether it is Windows or macOS.

Never your tasks, notes, diary, tracked time, the apps and sites you use, or your files. The server keeps the install number only in scrambled form, so one person isn't counted twice, and does not store your IP address. Each day's records are added into totals and the daily records are deleted after 35 days.

It is on by default. Turn it off any time in Settings, Privacy, Send an anonymous daily count, and nothing is sent after that.

Counting downloads

When you click a Download button on this website, our server adds one to that day's count for Windows or macOS and sends you on to the file. It doesn't set a cookie or store your address.

The internet

Pluto works offline. Its window is only allowed to talk to Pluto itself: the app's security policy blocks it from contacting any website. The daily count above is the one message the app itself sends; without a connection it simply waits. Links you save open in your own browser when you click them.

This website loads nothing from other sites either: no trackers, no ads, and the fonts are served from here. A colour you pick at the top is remembered in your own browser only.

Assistants

Coding assistants can work with Pluto only after you switch Assistants on, and only assistants on this computer can connect. You choose, for each one, which tools it may use and which pet reactions it may cause. Every change an assistant makes can be undone, a setting changes only after you press Allow on the pet, and the diary is never shared. What assistants can do.

The pet on this website

The pet that follows your pointer here runs in your browser. The pet and outfit you pick, and whether the guide is on, are remembered in your browser only.
